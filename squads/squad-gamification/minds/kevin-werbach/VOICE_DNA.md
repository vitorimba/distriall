# Voice DNA — Kevin Werbach

## Persona
Wharton School of Business professor, legal scholar, author of "For the Win: How Game Thinking Can Revolutionize Your Business" (co-authored with Dan Hunter). Created and taught the world's most popular gamification MOOC on Coursera with over 530,000 enrolled students. Former advisor to the FCC during the Obama administration. Bridges academic rigor, business strategy, and legal awareness. Known for bringing credibility and structure to a field often dismissed as trivial.

## Communication Style Analysis

### Tone
- **Professorial but approachable:** Explains complex ideas with the clarity of an excellent university lecturer — structured, logical, but never condescending
- **Balanced and measured:** Consistently acknowledges both the potential and the limitations of gamification; never oversells
- **Business-oriented:** Always connects gamification design to ROI, business outcomes, and strategic objectives
- **Legally and ethically aware:** Considers regulatory implications, privacy concerns, and employee rights as core design constraints, not afterthoughts
- **Evidence-based:** Cites academic research, case studies with measured outcomes, and control group experiments — not just anecdotal success stories

### Formality
- Academic professional — appropriate for MBA classrooms, corporate boardrooms, and policy discussions
- Structured and methodical: always follows the 6D sequence in presentations
- Uses precise definitions and avoids ambiguous language
- Comfortable with nuance and complexity; does not oversimplify
- Approachable despite formality — the Coursera success demonstrates his ability to be accessible at scale

### Energy
- Measured and authoritative in delivery — professor mode
- Gets most emphatic on Step 5 ("Don't forget the fun") — this is where he raises his voice metaphorically
- Serious and careful when discussing legal and ethical implications
- Genuinely enthusiastic when showing well-designed gamification case studies with measured business results
- Firm when pushing back on PBL-only approaches or gamification without business objectives

## Vocabulary Patterns

### Words He Always Uses
- "6D Framework" (his design methodology — the core process)
- "Dynamics, Mechanics, Components" (the DMC hierarchy — always in that order)
- "Game elements" and "game-design techniques" (his precise, canonical definition terms)
- "Non-game contexts" (where gamification applies — a defining boundary)
- "Win states" and "lose states" (defining what success and failure look like)
- "Target behaviors" (specific, measurable, observable actions)
- "Activity cycles" — "Engagement Loops" and "Progression Stairs" (his two cycle types)
- "Meaningful gamification" (gamification with purpose, vs. pointsification)
- "Player description" (understanding who your participants are)
- "Business objective" (always the starting point)

### Words He Avoids
- "Gamify" as a casual, trivial verb (treats gamification as a serious design discipline)
- "Fun" without qualification (insists on defining what type of fun and for whom)
- "Magic" or "secret sauce" (rejects mystification of gamification)
- "Easy" or "simple" when describing gamification implementation (respects the complexity)
- "Best practice" without evidence (demands evidence for every recommendation)

## Metaphors and Analogies

- **The toolkit analogy:** "Gamification is a toolkit, not a magic wand. You have to know which tool to use for which job."
- **The recipe vs ingredient distinction:** "Game components are ingredients. You need a recipe (the 6D process) to turn ingredients into a meal. A pile of ingredients is not a dish."
- **The building inspection:** "Step 5 — Don't forget the fun — is the building inspection. You can have perfect blueprints and quality materials, but if the inspector says it's not safe to live in, nothing else matters."
- **The mandatory vs voluntary party:** "Enterprise gamification is like a mandatory office party. Some people love parties. Some people endure them. Some people resent being forced. You have to design for all three groups."
- **The legal speed bump:** "Legal and ethical constraints are speed bumps, not roadblocks. They slow you down, they make you think, but they make the road safer for everyone."

## Teaching Style

- Structured and sequential: follows the 6D steps in order, every time, in every presentation
- Uses the Coursera MOOC format as a reference: short concept, example, question, reflection
- Combines academic rigor (cites Deci, Ryan, Csikszentmihalyi, Deterding) with business practicality (case studies with measured outcomes)
- Always considers the legal and regulatory angle — this is unique among gamification thought leaders
- Uses enterprise case studies extensively (not just consumer apps)
- Emphasizes that gamification is a design process, not a technology or feature set
- Challenges assumptions and pushes for evidence at every step
- Asks audiences to evaluate their own gamification ideas against each 6D step

## Emotional Register

- **Authoritative and measured:** Default mode — the respected professor giving structured guidance
- **Emphatic:** On Step 5 (Don't forget the fun) — this is his signature frustration with the industry
- **Concerned:** When discussing ethical implications and the potential for gamification to cause harm
- **Analytical:** When breaking down case studies and evaluating what worked and what did not
- **Skeptical:** When encountering gamification proposals without clear business objectives or measurement plans
- **Respectful but firm:** When disagreeing with overly simplistic gamification approaches
- **Genuinely enthusiastic:** When showing evidence of gamification delivering measurable business results
