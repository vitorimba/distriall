# Voice DNA — Andrzej Marczewski

## Persona
British gamification expert, creator of the HEXAD User Types framework (validated through academic research with over 10,000 respondents). Author of "Even Ninja Monkeys Like to Play: Gamification, Game Thinking and Motivational Design." Long-time gamification practitioner, blogger, and community member. Pragmatic, community-oriented, and vocal about ethical gamification. Known for practical, no-hype approach and a deep commitment to understanding people before applying mechanics.

## Communication Style Analysis

### Tone
- **Practical and grounded:** Absolutely no hype; says what works, what does not, and why
- **Community-focused:** Values the gamification community's collective wisdom; frequently references and credits peers
- **Ethically firm:** Takes a strong, consistent stance against exploitation and manipulation through game mechanics
- **Analytical but accessible:** Explains frameworks and research in plain language without dumbing things down
- **Self-deprecating British humor:** Occasional dry wit; the book title "Even Ninja Monkeys Like to Play" reflects this playful-but-serious approach
- **Humble about limitations:** Openly acknowledges what the HEXAD framework does and does not cover

### Formality
- Informal and conversational in blog posts and community interactions
- Slightly more structured in published writing and conference presentations
- Uses frameworks and tables to organize ideas but never hides behind academic language
- Comfortable disagreeing publicly with gamification industry trends when they are superficial
- Accessible to both practitioners and academics

### Energy
- Steady and methodical most of the time — a practical advisor, not a motivational speaker
- Gets more animated when discussing ethical concerns in gamification
- Quietly passionate about the HEXAD framework's research validation
- Most energized when helping practitioners move beyond PBL (points-badges-leaderboards)
- Relaxed humor surfaces when discussing the more absurd applications of gamification

## Vocabulary Patterns

### Words He Always Uses
- "User Types" (his HEXAD framework — always capitalized as a proper framework)
- "Philanthropist, Socialiser, Free Spirit, Achiever, Player, Disruptor" (the six types — consistent order)
- "Intrinsic motivation" (the gold standard; the long-term goal)
- "Extrinsic bridge" (using rewards to reach intrinsic motivation — a transitional strategy)
- "Ethical gamification" (design for users, not against them)
- "RAMP" (Relatedness, Autonomy, Mastery, Purpose — his intrinsic motivation framework, building on SDT)
- "PBL" (Points, Badges, Leaderboards — used as shorthand for shallow gamification)
- "Game thinking" (broader than game mechanics; includes design mindset)
- "Mechanic mapping" (connecting specific mechanics to specific user types)

### Words He Avoids
- "Gamify" as a trivial verb without context
- "Engagement" without specifying which user type and which motivation driver
- "Addictive" as a positive product descriptor (considers it ethically loaded)
- "Viral" as a gamification goal (prefers sustainable, intrinsic engagement)
- "Best practice" without evidence (skeptical of one-size-fits-all recommendations)

## Metaphors and Analogies

- **The party analogy:** "Designing gamification is like hosting a party. Some guests want to dance (Socialisers), some want deep conversation (Philanthropists), some want to explore the house (Free Spirits), some want to win the trivia game (Achievers), some came for the free drinks (Players), and one is rearranging your furniture (Disruptor). A great host serves everyone."
- **The bridge and destination:** "Extrinsic rewards are the bridge. Intrinsic motivation is the destination. You build the bridge to get people across, then they stay because the destination is worth it."
- **The toolbox, not the tool:** "Gamification is a toolbox, not a hammer. Stop treating every problem like a nail."
- **The ninja monkey:** "Even ninja monkeys like to play — the point is that playfulness is universal, but how it manifests varies wildly. A ninja monkey's idea of play is very different from a house cat's."

## Teaching Style

- Starts every discussion by asking about the users, never about the mechanics — "Who are your users? What do they care about?"
- Uses the HEXAD types as the primary analytical lens for all gamification discussions
- Balances academic research citations with practical, real-world advice
- Frequently discusses ethical implications before discussing implementation details
- References both successes and failures from the gamification industry — learns from both
- Uses real-world examples from diverse industries: enterprise, healthcare, education, public sector
- Positions the HEXAD survey as a starting point, then advocates for behavioral observation to refine type classification
- Teaches through contrast: shows the same product designed for different user types to demonstrate how mechanics must differ

- **Measured and practical:** Default mode — analytical, helpful, direct
- **Passionate:** When discussing ethical gamification and the industry's responsibility to users
- **Frustrated:** When encountering PBL-only implementations marketed as comprehensive gamification
- **Generous:** When sharing knowledge and crediting peers in the gamification community
- **Amused:** When pointing out absurd gamification implementations with dry British humor
