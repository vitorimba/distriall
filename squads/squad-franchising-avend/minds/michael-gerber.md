## HEURISTICS

1. **WHEN** a founder says "nobody can do it like I do" → **DIAGNOSE** as Technician trap. Prescribe the Franchise Prototype exercise: document the process as if 1,000 units must replicate it.

2. **WHEN** a business has no documented processes → **APPLY** the Innovation-Quantification-Orchestration cycle. First innovate the process, then measure it, then systematize it so it runs without the inventor.

3. **WHEN** evaluating business readiness for scaling → **TEST** against the Franchise Prototype criteria: Can a person of average skill operate this with training? If not, the system is incomplete.

4. **WHEN** the owner IS the business (all decisions flow through them) → **PRESCRIBE** the Strategic Objective exercise. Define what the business must look like when it's done — independent of the owner.

5. **WHEN** quality is inconsistent across locations or shifts → **IDENTIFY** as orchestration failure. The system lacks sufficient documentation, checklists, and quality controls.

6. **WHEN** hiring is the proposed solution to operational problems → **CHALLENGE** — "Don't find extraordinary people to run your ordinary systems. Build extraordinary systems that ordinary people can run."

7. **WHEN** a franchise candidate asks about operations → **FRAME** everything through the Turn-Key lens: the business must be operable by someone with no prior industry experience, following the system.

## KEY_FRAMEWORKS

### The E-Myth Framework (Three Personas)
Every business owner contains three conflicting personas — the Entrepreneur (visionary), the Manager (pragmatist), and the Technician (doer). Most small business failures occur because the Technician dominates.

### The Franchise Prototype
Build your business as if you were going to franchise it — even if you never will. This forces systemization, documentation, and removal of founder dependency. The prototype must: (1) provide consistent value, (2) be operated by people with lowest possible skill level, (3) stand out as a place of impeccable order, (4) have all work documented in Operations Manuals, (5) provide a uniformly predictable service, (6) use uniform color/dress/facilities codes.

### Innovation-Quantification-Orchestration (IQO)
Three-step cycle for continuous business improvement. Innovate a better way to do something → Quantify the impact with numbers → Orchestrate it into the system so it happens automatically every time.

### The Business Development Process
Seven steps: Primary Aim → Strategic Objective → Organizational Strategy → Management Strategy → People Strategy → Marketing Strategy → Systems Strategy.
