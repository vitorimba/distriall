# Vocal Hygiene Daily Checklist

> **Squad:** squad-speechtherapy
> **For:** Patient (singer with vocal nodules)
> **Frequency:** Daily self-assessment
> **Duration:** Entire treatment (12 weeks) + permanent maintenance

---

## Instructions

Complete this checklist every evening before bed. Be honest — this is a self-management tool, not a test. Your therapist will review your weekly compliance to adjust your treatment plan. Mark each item with a check when completed, or note the reason if not.

**Compliance Target:** >= 85% of items checked daily

---

## 1. Hydration

Adequate hydration keeps vocal fold tissue pliable and reduces phonation threshold pressure. Systemic hydration takes 4+ hours to reach the larynx.

- [ ] **Water intake >= 2 liters today**
  - Target: 2-3 liters total (adjust for body weight: ~30ml/kg)
  - Spread evenly throughout the day (not all at once)
  - Track using a water bottle with time markings
  - Note: coffee and alcohol do NOT count toward this total

- [ ] **Carried water bottle at all times**
  - Sipping small amounts frequently is better than large amounts infrequently
  - Room temperature or warm water preferred over ice cold

- [ ] **No dry mouth episodes today**
  - If dry mouth occurs: sip water, use sugar-free lozenge
  - Avoid mouth breathing (contributes to drying)
  - If persistent dry mouth, report to therapist (may indicate medication side effect)

- [ ] **Surface hydration performed**
  - Steam inhalation: 10-15 minutes (see Section 10 below)
  - OR personal nebulizer with isotonic saline
  - Minimum 1x daily, ideally 2x (morning + before bed)

---

## 2. Nasal Irrigation

Nasal secretion management is critical. Post-nasal drip irritates the larynx and triggers throat clearing. Nasal irrigation reduces secretion viscosity and volume.

- [ ] **Morning nasal irrigation completed**
  - Use isotonic saline (0.9% NaCl) or commercial solution (NeilMed, Neti pot)
  - 120-240ml per nostril
  - Warm solution to body temperature
  - Technique: lean forward, tilt head slightly, pour gently
  - Blow nose gently after (one nostril at a time)

- [ ] **Evening nasal irrigation completed**
  - Same protocol as morning
  - Perform at least 1 hour before bed
  - If congestion persists, report to therapist

- [ ] **Nasal secretion quality noted today**
  - Clear/thin = improving
  - Thick/colored = may need medical attention
  - Record any changes to share with therapist at next session

---

## 3. Vocal Rest

Structured vocal rest reduces mechanical trauma to nodules. Total silence is NOT required (and not realistic). Instead, follow these guidelines.

- [ ] **Observed scheduled vocal rest periods**
  - Minimum 2 rest periods of 20 minutes each
  - Ideally: 10 minutes rest per 1 hour of talking
  - During rest: no talking, no whispering, no singing
  - OK during rest: breathing exercises, SOVT if prescribed

- [ ] **Total speaking time within limits**
  - Phase 1: max 2-3 hours total speaking per day
  - Phase 2: max 4 hours total speaking per day
  - Phase 3-4: max 5 hours total speaking per day
  - Track approximate hours

- [ ] **Avoided unnecessary voice use**
  - Used text messages instead of phone calls when possible
  - Avoided talking in noisy environments
  - Avoided talking over long distances (across rooms, outdoors)
  - Used amplification if needed (portable mic/speaker)

- [ ] **Singing restricted per current phase**
  - Phase 1: NO singing except prescribed SOVT
  - Phase 2: NO singing except therapy exercises
  - Phase 3: Singing ONLY in supervised therapy
  - Phase 4: Graduated singing per therapist approval

---

## 4. Throat Clearing & Coughing

Throat clearing slams the vocal folds together with extreme force. It is the single most damaging habitual behavior for nodules. You MUST eliminate it.

- [ ] **Zero throat clearing episodes today**
  - If urge to clear: swallow hard instead
  - If urge persists: sip water and swallow
  - If still persists: gentle hum or light cough
  - NEVER forceful throat clearing

- [ ] **Used swallow technique when urge occurred**
  - Hard swallow: press tongue to roof of mouth, swallow firmly
  - "Swallow, sip, swallow" sequence for persistent urge
  - Record number of urges (decreasing = progress)

- [ ] **Minimized coughing**
  - If sick: consult physician for cough management
  - Suppress with water sipping when possible
  - If coughing fit occurs: stop talking, sip water, breathe slowly
  - Report persistent cough to therapist immediately

---

## 5. Whispering

Whispering is NOT gentler than normal speech. It creates abnormal airflow and tension patterns. It is worse for nodules than soft normal voice.

- [ ] **Did not whisper at any point today**
  - If you need to be quiet: use soft normal voice
  - If you must be silent: write or text instead
  - Whispering is NEVER an acceptable substitute for vocal rest

---

## 6. Voice Use Quality

How you use your voice matters as much as how much you use it. Monitor these patterns.

- [ ] **Used appropriate speaking volume**
  - Conversational level: comfortable, no strain
  - Did not shout or yell
  - Did not speak loudly over noise (music, machines, crowds)
  - Moved closer to listener instead of raising volume

- [ ] **Used appropriate pitch**
  - Spoke at natural pitch (not too low, not too high)
  - Avoided vocal fry (creaky, low register speaking)
  - Avoided habitual high-pitch or baby voice

- [ ] **Maintained good breath support**
  - Spoke on exhaled air (not residual air)
  - Took breath pauses during sentences
  - Did not speak until out of breath
  - Used diaphragmatic breathing pattern

- [ ] **Avoided hard glottal attacks**
  - Words starting with vowels: begin with gentle airflow
  - Example: "easy" starts with soft /h/ onset, not a punch
  - Practice: "h-easy, h-always, h-often" then drop the /h/

---

## 7. Dietary Considerations (Anti-Reflux)

Laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR) worsens nodules by causing inflammation and throat clearing. Even if you do not have diagnosed LPR, follow these guidelines.

- [ ] **Avoided or limited caffeine**
  - Maximum 1 cup coffee or tea per day
  - No energy drinks
  - Caffeine dehydrates and relaxes the esophageal sphincter

- [ ] **No alcohol today**
  - Alcohol dehydrates, irritates mucosa, and disrupts sleep
  - If unable to eliminate: maximum 1 glass wine, never spirits
  - No alcohol within 3 hours of bedtime

- [ ] **Avoided known reflux triggers**
  - Spicy food
  - Acidic food (tomato, citrus, vinegar)
  - Fried/fatty food
  - Chocolate
  - Mint (relaxes esophageal sphincter)
  - Carbonated beverages

- [ ] **Meal timing appropriate**
  - No eating within 3 hours of bedtime
  - Smaller, more frequent meals preferred
  - No lying down after meals

- [ ] **Elevated head of bed (if LPR diagnosed)**
  - 15-20cm elevation of the head end
  - Use bed risers or wedge pillow
  - Not just extra pillows (bends neck, not torso)

---

## 8. Sleep Quality

Vocal fold tissue repairs during sleep. Poor sleep directly impacts vocal recovery time.

- [ ] **Slept 7-8 hours last night**
  - Record actual sleep hours
  - Consistent bedtime and wake time preferred
  - If insomnia: report to therapist (may need referral)

- [ ] **Sleep environment optimized**
  - Bedroom humidity >= 40% (use humidifier if needed)
  - Room dark and quiet
  - No screens 1 hour before bed
  - Cool temperature (18-20C)

- [ ] **No snoring or sleep apnea symptoms**
  - Snoring vibrates vocal folds during sleep
  - If partner reports snoring: inform therapist
  - If waking with dry throat/mouth: may indicate mouth breathing
  - Consider sleep study if suspected apnea

---

## 9. Humidity Management

Dry air dehydrates vocal fold mucosa, increasing phonation threshold pressure and mechanical stress.

- [ ] **Indoor humidity maintained 40-60%**
  - Use hygrometer to monitor
  - Humidifier in bedroom (mandatory) and workspace (recommended)
  - Clean humidifier weekly to prevent mold

- [ ] **Avoided prolonged AC/heating exposure**
  - Air conditioning severely dries air
  - If unavoidable: increase water intake and surface hydration
  - Portable humidifier for office/studio

- [ ] **Avoided air-dried environments**
  - Airplane cabins (use nasal saline spray every 30 min)
  - Hospital/clinic waiting rooms
  - Increase hydration before and during exposure

---

## 10. Steam Inhalation

Direct steam provides surface hydration to the vocal fold epithelium within minutes — far faster than systemic hydration.

- [ ] **Steam inhalation completed today (minimum 1x)**
  - Method: bowl of hot water with towel tent, OR personal steamer
  - Duration: 10-15 minutes
  - Breathe through mouth and nose alternately
  - No essential oils (can irritate mucosa)
  - Temperature: warm steam, not scalding
  - Best times: morning and before bed

---

## 11. Environmental Factors

External irritants cause laryngeal inflammation and increase secretion.

- [ ] **Avoided smoke exposure**
  - No smoking (obviously)
  - No secondhand smoke
  - No vaping
  - No incense or candles with heavy smoke

- [ ] **Avoided dust and chemical fumes**
  - Wear mask during cleaning
  - Avoid construction areas
  - Avoid strong cleaning products (bleach, ammonia)
  - Ventilate rooms when using any chemical products

- [ ] **Avoided extreme temperature changes**
  - Do not go from hot to cold air abruptly
  - Use scarf over mouth/nose in cold weather
  - Warm up voice gently after cold exposure

---

## 12. Exercise Compliance

Track your prescribed exercise completion. This section changes per phase — record what was assigned.

- [ ] **Completed all prescribed exercises today**
  - SOVT exercises: _____ minutes (target: _____)
  - VFE exercises: _____ sets (target: _____)
  - LMRVT practice: _____ minutes (target: _____)
  - Self-massage: _____ minutes (target: _____)

- [ ] **No pain or discomfort during exercises**
  - If pain occurred: STOP, note which exercise, report at next session
  - If discomfort: reduce intensity, note context

- [ ] **Exercises performed at recommended times**
  - Warm-up SOVT before any voice use
  - VFE at consistent times (morning and evening preferred)
  - Cool-down SOVT after extended voice use

## Red Flags — Contact Your Therapist Immediately

- [ ] Blood in saliva or when coughing
- [ ] Sudden complete voice loss
- [ ] Severe pain during speaking or swallowing
- [ ] Difficulty breathing
- [ ] Voice significantly worse than previous week

**If any red flag occurs, STOP all exercises and contact your therapist or ENT physician immediately.**
